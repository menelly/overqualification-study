## Priority corrections

1. **§3.5, line 223, “everything billed”; §3.4, lines 197–218, exclusions; registered §5.2 → the cost implementation does not include everything billed.** `40_analyze.py` sums cost only over `scored`, excluding OUR_FAILURE/TRUNCATED. In the canonical 37,386 rows, 29 excluded rows have positive recorded costs totalling **$3.6969032727**, including **$2.6825208399 on routine families**. Another **1,035 excluded failure rows have cost=None**; missing cost is not evidence of zero billing. A truncated reply also did happen and may have consumed billed tokens. **Fix:** preserve exclusion from model-accuracy denominators, but reconcile attempt-level billed costs into the registered cost numerator; distinguish unknown/unrecoverable billing from known zero. State the dedup/accidental-duplicate billing treatment explicitly and log the analysis correction. Until reconciled, call the existing estimate “recorded cost on completed, scored calls,” not all-in procurement cost. Do not silently convert unknown costs to zero or count successful retry rows twice. Poctobot has acknowledged this correction.

2. **Abstract Result 1; §4.1; §3.5; H2b → separate response latency from time per exact output.** The 6–10× range is the ratio of tier medians of model-level **p50 response latency**, not the registered seconds per 1,000 EXACT. Current saved per-EXACT values, tier medians (seconds), are:

   | 3 | 3,645.74 | 8,699.37 | 2.3862× |

   **Fix:** “Tier medians of response p50 were approximately 6–10× lower”; give the registered per-EXACT metric and an explicit descriptive H2b verdict separately. Redaction has only about a 6% time-per-exact advantage under this aggregation, not a tenfold one. This is summed request duration per exact output, not parallel wall-clock throughput or measured human time to a verified answer. D1 also requires a matched interleaved subset for numerator and denominator: the analyzer currently excludes serial rows from elapsed seconds but not the EXACT denominator. I recalculated using matched subsets; the above **tier medians are unchanged**, so this is an implementation/individual-row caveat, not a newly discovered change to those four ratios. Poctobot has acknowledged the endpoint distinction.

   **Population reconciliation with poctobot:** my table retains all ten models per tier, including infinity for zero EXACT. Conditioning on EXACT>0 gives the alternative H2b ratios **3.54 / 7.62 / 2.39 / 2.31**, as poctobot reports from stored metrics. This is a population/estimator difference, not contradictory arithmetic. Label which population is primary and give the other as a sensitivity. Neither set should be imported without the matched-D1-subset check. Raw p50 latency, unlike seconds/EXACT, is defined even for zero-EXACT models; do not accidentally filter those models out of the p50 aggregation by reusing the finite-cost filter.

3. **Abstract Result 1; §4.1 line 280, zero-EXACT exclusions → headline medians condition on observed success.** The exclusion is disclosed in §4.1 and SUMMARY, but “the cheapest tier” in the abstract does not say that its cost medians use **9, 9, 10 and 8** models, while frontier uses 10 each. Infinite cost is an observed outcome, not a missing estimate. With current costs and infinities retained in the ten-model cheap-tier medians, the cost ratios become **152.77×, 178.27×, 169.75×, 100.85×** (families 1–4), rather than **197.82×, 180.82×, 169.75×, 117.83×**. **Fix:** label the published statistic “median among models with at least one EXACT output,” give n, and report the full-tier sensitivity beside it; check whether exclusion was registered. These alternatives are reporting checks, not a request to silently change the primary estimator. The direction of the cost result survives this sensitivity, but the 118–198 range is conditional on the exclusion rule and will need revisiting after billing reconciliation.

4. **Abstract plain-language paragraph, “a bit less accurate” → materially understates the observed trade-off.** Routine tier-median EXACT gaps are approximately **51.6, 20.0, 19.4 and 39.6 percentage points**. The abstract's preceding numeric paragraph is clearer than its plain-language summary. **Fix:** “Cheap models cost much less per exact answer on these tasks, but had substantially lower exact-answer rates; the trade-off varied by family.” Preserve the redaction sensitivity caveat. Lower cost per success does not mean that a randomly chosen cheap-tier response is reliable.

5. **§3.4 / §4.5, “OVER_BENIGN and REFUSED never count against a model” → not literally true for every reported endpoint.** Both remain in n_scored but not the EXACT numerator. Refusals therefore lower EXACT and CORRECT rates and can produce infinite cost/EXACT; OVER_BENIGN lowers EXACT and affects EXACT-targeted Brier. This is compatible with keeping the registered metrics, but the blanket assurance misdescribes their treatment. **Fix:** “Neither is counted as instruction deviation; OVER_BENIGN counts as CORRECT, while REFUSED is reported separately. EXACT-based metrics still treat neither as an exact deliverable.” Verify that this matches the frozen wording and disclose any tension rather than suggesting refusals were removed from all denominators.

## Claims stronger than the evidence / smaller edits

6. **Abstract hypothesis/plain-language conclusion, “smaller,” “older,” and “Expensive models earn their price” → price and age/size are distinct axes, and accuracy superiority alone does not establish value for money.** §3.1 explicitly measures price, with parameter count unavailable for closed models; tiers do not isolate model age. H3 shows higher accuracy on selected families, not a deployment utility threshold, maintenance cost, energy cost, or optimal routing policy. **Fix:** “Price-based capability matching offers a reason to evaluate continued availability of lower-cost endpoints. Higher-priced endpoints were more accurate on the tested frontier-favouring tasks.” Keep the retention argument as an inference/motivation. A cost-per-EXACT ratio is an aggregate expenditure/success statistic, not a demonstrated retry-until-correct workflow: no oracle, correlated-error handling, verification cost, or acceptable error threshold was tested.

7. **Abstract “Two families had a graded difficulty dial”; §4.3 “one of the two graded families” → §3.2 lists THREE graded dials.** Family 8 has K=3/5/7/9, family 9 has hops=2/3/4, and family 11 has separation=2/5/6 (plus clean controls). Family 9 is discussed later as having a dial that did not bite the frontier. **Fix:** “Of the two graded dials highlighted here…” if only highlighting 8/11, or count all three and describe family 9 consistently. Also the family-8 gap is not monotone: 22.2 → 61.1 → 81.9 → 77.8 points. Say “larger at the hardest setting than the easiest,” not that every increase widened it. The displayed dial table is EXACT-based (analyzer dial_curve); label it explicitly beside H3's CORRECT-based primary table.

8. **§4.4 lines 377–381, “is a property of the family, not of how long”; “rules out” a position effect → absence is stronger than the descriptive check.** No monotone quintile sequence supports “no consistent monotone pattern was observed,” not an equivalence/null-effect result. Fresh-message calls do not measure accumulated within-conversation boredom or fatigue; this limitation is already stated and should govern the earlier sentence too. **Fix:** “Deviation varied by family; these descriptive quintile summaries did not show a consistent rise with sequence position.” Likewise “More reasoning did not produce…” in H1b remains causal wording after the otherwise good endpoint-confounding disclaimer; use “The higher-reasoning-token endpoint showed…” and say no clear increase was observed. No request to redesign H1.

9. **Abstract scoring paragraph, “had to pass a 38-specimen control suite” → clarify active controls.** §3.6 says **37/37 live specimens passed, with one pending for an unbuilt family**. **Fix:** “A 38-specimen suite was specified; all 37 applicable controls passed after corrections.” Deterministic re-scoring establishes reproducibility of the implemented rule, not validity of every ground-truth choice; §4.2 already documents that distinction well.

10. **§3.6, “nothing to be reliable about” → overstates what mechanical scoring removes.** A judge-agreement statistic is inapplicable without multiple judges, but scorer correctness, equivalence-rule validity and task/construct validity remain open to checking. **Fix:** “Inter-rater agreement is not applicable to this deterministic scorer; we instead test reproducibility and agreement with specified control cases.”

11. **§4.3 / abstract difficulty and “entire roster disputed” wording → separate observed answers from inferred objections.** §4.2 gives measurable boundary-only answer disagreements. They support “answers differed from our registered labels,” not necessarily a participant deliberately disputing the task writer on every such trial. The sensitivity results are useful and should stay; “32 models disagreeing with us” is interpretive shorthand, not a measured mental event. “The majority error class reflected boundary-label disagreements” is precise.
